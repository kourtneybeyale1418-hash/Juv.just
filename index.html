Juvenile Justice & Mental Health

San Juan County, New Mexico

This project explains how poverty, brain development, trauma, and correctional systems affect youth mental health.

San Juan County, NM

• Rural region in northwestern New Mexico
• Includes part of Navajo Nation
• Poverty ~19–20% (higher for children)

Poverty & Chronic Stress

Poverty creates constant stress from housing instability, food insecurity, and financial pressure.

This leads to toxic stress, which affects brain development over time.

Brain Development Effects

Prefrontal Cortex: decision-making & impulse control

Hippocampus: memory & learning

Amygdala: fear & emotional response

Trauma in Youth

Many justice-involved youth experience:

• Abuse
• Neglect
• Community violence
• Family instability

Trauma increases risk of PTSD and emotional dysregulation.

Juvenile Justice System

Detention is not therapy.

Without treatment, youth often experience:

• Increased depression
• Anxiety
• Self-harm risk
• Recidivism (returning to system)

Correctional Officers (COs)

COs shape daily environment in detention centers.

Harsh interactions can trigger trauma responses.

Supportive interactions can improve emotional regulation.

Pathway Into Justice System

Poverty → Chronic stress → Brain changes → School issues → Juvenile justice involvement

Solutions

• Trauma-informed care
• Mental health access in rural areas
• School counseling
• Diversion programs (avoid detention)
